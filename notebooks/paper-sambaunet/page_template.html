SAMBA-UNet, explained step by step

arXiv 2610.40029 · "Segment Anything Model Based Context Guided Mamba Unet for Medical Image Segmentation" · Wang & Zhou, Cardiff University · 29 Oct 2026
Read this first. This is an unofficial explainer. The paper says "code will be released upon acceptance", so there is no official repo: every line of code on this page was written by us from the paper's equations and Figure 1, and every guess is marked our assumption. Real = SAM ViT-B weights (facebook/sam-vit-base) and the numbers captured from running our code. Stand-in = the Mamba branch is randomly initialised (the paper starts from VMamba-S weights), and the selective scan is plain PyTorch. Nothing here reproduces the paper's accuracy numbers.

The whole idea in one minute

The problem. A skin-lesion or organ boundary is a low-contrast edge. CNNs (U-Net) see only locally. Transformers see globally but cost O(N²). Mamba (state-space models) sees globally at O(N) but has no "what is an object" knowledge. SAM knows objects, but medical images look nothing like its training photos.
The trick. Run both on the same image. Fine-tune SAM cheaply (LoRA + Adapters, 2.5 M of 121 M params). Then at three depths hand SAM's features to the Mamba U-Net decoder through a small attention gate (HCEM). SAM says what, Mamba says where exactly. No prompts needed.
The claim. Synapse DSC 81.94 (VM-UNet 81.08), HD95 17.34 (19.21); ISIC17 mIoU 81.07 (80.23); ISIC18 mIoU 81.69 (81.35). Gains of 0.2–0.9 points; no seeds or error bars reported — see "What the paper does not say" below.

3 · Interactive: why Mamba scans in four directions

SS2D flattens the 16×16 grid (here: the paper's lesion image) into a sequence, in 4 orders. A state-space model reads the sequence once and keeps a running memory. In one order, a pixel never sees what comes after it. The decay below is illustrative (real decay is learned and input-dependent — that is the "selective" in S6).

7 · How a data scientist implements this

  1. Load SAM ViT-B from transformers, freeze it, take the vision encoder only (no prompt encoder / mask decoder needed — that is the "prompt-free" claim).
  2. Feed it 256×256. HF's patch embedding refuses anything but 1024 — call the conv patch_embed.projection directly and interpolate the absolute position embedding 64×64 → 16×16. our assumption
  3. Wrap each block: LoRA on the fused qkv (Q and V slices only, zero-init up-projection), Adapter after attention and in the MLP path (zero-init). Check: output must equal plain SAM at step 0 — we got max diff 0.00e+00.
  4. Tap blocks 6, 9, 12 (the global-attention blocks) → three (B,768,16,16) maps.
  5. Build the VSS U-Net (96-192-384-768, two VSS blocks per stage). Use VMamba's official selective_scan CUDA kernel for speed and load VMamba-S weights (vmamba_small_e238_ema.pth, as the paper does). We used a chunked PyTorch scan to stay dependency-free: ≈25× faster backward than a naive loop (282 s → 11.5 s) and equal to it up to 1.4e-6.
  6. HCEM ×3 at (S6,M1), (S9,M2), (S12,M3); fuse into the decoder.
  7. Loss BCE+Dice (binary: ISIC) or CE+Dice (Synapse); AdamW lr 1e-3, cosine to 1e-4, batch 32, 256×256 — all from §4.2.
  8. Sanity-check before any real run: (a) identity at init, (b) gradients non-zero where expected and zero on frozen SAM, (c) overfit one batch to Dice ≈ 1. Then train; report mean ± std over seeds (the paper does not).
Compute we measured: 121.0 M params (89.7 M frozen SAM, 2.5 M CoSE, 28.8 M Mamba U-Net + HCEM); batch-2 forward 5.9 s / backward 11.5 s with the pure-PyTorch scan, peak 21 GB on a GB10. The paper claims "computational efficiency" but prints no parameter, FLOP or speed numbers.

8 · What the paper does not say (or gets inconsistent)

  • No code, no weights ("upon acceptance").
  • Names: SAMBA-UNet / SAMamba-UNet / SAMambUnet; HCEM in text, CPEM in Fig. 1; "SKBS" is a leftover from another paper in §3.3.
  • Table 3: header arrows swapped. Text says LoRA "reduces HD95 by 6.2%" — 29.89 → 23.69 is 6.2 points (≈21%); "HD95 of 23.18%" is not a percentage.
  • Ablation baseline (a) is undefined and scores 79.05, below VM-UNet's 81.08 in Table 2. There is no HCEM-only row, so we cannot tell how much of the gain is SAM's features and how much is HCEM itself.
  • Text vs tables: prose cites VM-UNet "only 82% DSC", "ACM-UNet 85.12%" and TransUNet "89.8% Dice on ISIC-17", none of which are in Tables 1–2 (VM-UNet is 81.08 there).
  • Citation: "MedSAM [41]" — reference [41] is Medical SAM Adapter (same as [31]), not MedSAM.
  • Unspecified: SAM input size/position handling, LoRA rank, adapter width, λ values, how multi-scale transform MS(·) works beyond "upsample", where exactly the Adapters add, decoder fusion (concat vs add), whether SAM's mask decoder was used in the reported numbers.
  • Statistics: single numbers, no seeds, no confidence intervals; the gains over VM-UNet are 0.2–0.9 points.
