Look Only When Needed: Certificate-Guided Visual Routing for Efficient Diffusion VLMs

arXiv 2610.36671 · picked for the week of 2026-10-30 · Offers a certificate-guided visual routing technique to reduce inference latency for diffusion-based VLMs, which is crucial for deploying efficient, real-time defect detection systems on local hardware.

The paper introduces LOWN, a decoding method for diffusion vision-language models that decides at each step whether to process the image based on prediction agreement. It retains 99.6% of the baseline score on ten benchmarks while using only 35.3% of the decoding FLOPs.

Explain it simply

Explain it like I'm five

Imagine you are drawing a picture, but you keep looking at a reference photo every time you pick up your pencil. This paper says you don't need to look at the photo every single time. Sometimes, you can just trust your memory of what you saw before. If your memory matches what you are about to draw, you can keep drawing without looking. If you are not sure, then you look at the photo again. This way, you finish your drawing much faster because you are not constantly checking the photo. It works like a smart shortcut for computers that look at pictures and write about them.

Explain it like I'm a data scientist

Diffusion vision-language models (DVLMs) like LLaDA-V process the entire input, including visual tokens, at every denoising step. Since visual tokens constitute 89–97% of the input, most computation is wasted repeatedly processing the same image. Existing methods like visual token pruning reduce the number of tokens but still process the remaining image at every step, and they lose information. The gap is that there is no mechanism to determine if the image is actually needed for the current decoding decision.

Visual tokens

Anchor

Certificate

Unmasking
Revealing a hidden letter in a word puzzle one by one. The process of replacing a masked placeholder with a specific predicted token in the response sequence.

Run a FULL forward to establish the initial anchor

At the start of decoding or when a new anchor is needed, the model processes the full input including visual tokens. It stores the top-1 predictions and confidences for all masked positions as the anchor.

Execute a TEXT-ONLY forward to test the anchor

At each subsequent step, the model runs a cheaper forward pass without visual tokens. It compares the current text-only predictions against the stored anchor predictions to check for agreement.

Identify the longest prefix of certified tokens

The model scans the masked positions ordered by anchor confidence. It marks tokens as certified if the text-only prediction matches the anchor. The scan stops at the first disagreement, defining the certified prefix.

Determine if the certified prefix is sufficient

The length of the certified prefix is compared to the baseline unmasking budget k. If the prefix is long enough, the step proceeds without a FULL forward; otherwise, a FULL forward is triggered.

Unmask tokens or refresh the anchor

If certified, the model unmasks the entire certified prefix to the anchor predictions. If not, it runs a FULL forward, updates the anchor with new predictions, and unmasks k tokens prioritizing those where text-only and full predictions disagreed.
