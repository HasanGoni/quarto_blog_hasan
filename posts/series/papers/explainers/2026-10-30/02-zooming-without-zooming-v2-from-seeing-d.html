Explain it simply

Explain it like I'm five

Imagine you are looking at a busy playground picture. To find the red ball, you have to look at the slide, the swing, and the sandbox all at once. This paper teaches computers to do that same job. It makes up tricky questions that force the computer to look at different parts of a picture together. It checks if the computer is really looking at the right spots by hiding some parts. If the computer can still answer, it wasn't paying attention! This helps the computer get better at seeing details and understanding how things fit together.

Explain it like I'm a data scientist

The core problem is that high-quality training data for spatial perception and multimodal long-context understanding is expensive and difficult to synthesize. Existing methods often struggle with reliable labeling or fail to generalize because they focus on specific task formats rather than the underlying cognitive capability required to bind scattered visual facts to entities and relations.

The proposed method, EVIDENCE CONNECT, addresses this by targeting the atomic capability of 'connecting evidence.' It automatically synthesizes compositional questions from single images by selecting groups of related regions and generating queries that require aggregation, comparison, or chaining of facts from these regions. The pipeline uses privileged zoomed views for question generation but trains the model on the full image with region outlines, ensuring the model must perceive and connect evidence independently. Quality is enforced via cross-model answer agreement and adaptive drop-one region ablation tests to verify that the answer depends on all selected regions.

Connecting Evidence
Like solving a puzzle where you must link a clue on one piece to a picture on another to find the final answer. The atomic capability of identifying visual facts, binding them to entities, recovering relations, and composing them into an answer.
Privileged Evidence Layout
A cheat sheet that shows you the close-up details of specific puzzle pieces before you have to solve the whole board. A high-resolution crop view of selected regions used only during data synthesis to help generate accurate questions, not shown to the model during training.
RLVR
A teacher who only gives you a gold star if your final answer is exactly right, making you practice until you get it. Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards, a training method where the model is rewarded only when it produces a correct, verifiable answer.
Region Ablation
Covering up one part of a map to see if you still know where you are; if you do, that part wasn't important. A test where specific image regions are masked to verify that the question's answer actually depends on those regions.
Cross-model Answer Anchoring
Asking two different friends to solve a riddle and only keeping it if they both agree on the same answer. A verification step where VLMs from different families must agree on the reference answer for a synthesized question to be kept.
Compositional Question
A question that requires mixing ingredients from three different bowls to bake a cake, rather than just tasting one bowl. A question whose answer depends on combining information from multiple distinct regions or entities.

Identifying high-resolution images suitable for multi-region questions

The pipeline starts by filtering for high-resolution, visually complex images. A strong VLM judges if the image can support compositional questions and proposes concrete, localizable regions with bounding boxes and descriptions.

Selecting coherent groups of regions that support a single question

Proposed regions are grouped based on task families and necessity modes. Invalid or duplicated groups are discarded, ensuring that the selected regions jointly support one specific compositional question type like aggregation or comparison.

Creating compositional questions using privileged zoomed views

A privileged evidence layout is constructed with native-resolution crops of the selected regions. A VLM uses this layout to generate candidate questions with concise answers and reasoning certificates, ensuring the questions rely on the specific evidence in the group.

Validating reference answers through cross-model majority agreement

Two VLMs from different families answer the question from the privileged layout. The candidate is kept only if a clear majority of responses from each model supports the same answer, which becomes the reference answer.

Ensuring the answer depends on all selected regions via drop-one tests

Each region is masked in the privileged layout to test if the answer survives. If the answer remains correct without a region, it is marked as redundant. Questions with more than one redundant region are discarded to ensure strict evidence dependence.

Converting verified questions to multiple-choice format for RLVR

Verified questions are paired with the full image (with region outlines) and converted into four-option multiple-choice questions. Distractors are added, and overly easy examples are filtered out to provide a strong learning signal for reinforcement learning.
