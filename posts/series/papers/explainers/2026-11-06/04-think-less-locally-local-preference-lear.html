Think Less, Locally: Local Preference Learning for Reasoning Compression

arXiv 2611.04814 · picked for the week of 2026-11-06 · Local Preference Learning for Reasoning Compression helps reduce inference costs and latency, making it feasible to deploy fine-tuned VLMs on local hardware for real-time defect detection.

arXiv HF paper page Code

LoCo is a method that teaches AI models to stop reasoning when they already have enough information, rather than just forcing them to write shorter answers. By identifying specific 'overthinking' moments in the model's own thought process and training it to prefer shorter, correct continuations from those exact points, LoCo reduces reasoning length by up to 45% on math and 36% on code without losing accuracy.

Explain it simply

Explain it like I'm five

Imagine you are solving a puzzle and you already found the right piece, but you keep looking for more pieces just because you like looking. This paper teaches the computer to stop looking once it has the right piece. It looks at the computer's own notes to see where it starts repeating itself. Then, it shows the computer a shorter way to finish the puzzle from that exact spot. The computer learns to say, 'I am done thinking,' instead of 'I need to think more.' This makes the computer faster and saves energy. It works for math problems and even for writing computer code, even though it only practiced on math.

Explain it like I'm a data scientist

Large reasoning models often suffer from 'overthinking,' where they continue generating tokens after the answer is already derivable, incurring high inference costs without accuracy gains. Existing compression methods typically operate at the trajectory level (comparing full short vs. long solutions) or by removing specific redundant steps, but they fail to capture the local decision of *when* to stop reasoning given the current state. This gap means models may still overthink on hard problems or under-think on easy ones because the compression signal is not state-conditional.

LoCo addresses this by constructing state-conditioned preference pairs. It first detects 'overthinking states' in self-generated traces using a two-stage filter: a confidence/volatility screen followed by a judge model that verifies if the next step is redundant. From these frozen states, it regenerates shorter, verified continuations. The model is then trained with DPO to prefer these shorter continuations over the original longer ones from the same prefix, effectively learning a local 'continue-or-conclude' policy that generalizes across domains.

Re-checking your work on a math problem you already solved correctly, just to be safe. The tendency of a model to continue reasoning beyond the point where sufficient information has been obtained to reach a correct answer.
DPO (Direct Preference Optimization)
Teaching a student by showing them two answers and saying, 'This one is better than that one,' rather than giving a strict rule. A training method that aligns a model with human preferences by optimizing the likelihood of preferred responses over dispreferred ones.

Identifying candidate overthinking states via confidence metrics

The model scores its own chain-of-thought to calculate step-level confidence and volatility. States with low confidence and high volatility are flagged as potential overthinking points. This initial screen uses only the model's internal probabilities to narrow down candidates.

Confirming overthinking with a stronger judge model

A separate judge model reviews the flagged states to determine if the next step is truly redundant (OVERTHINK) or necessary (NORMAL/FINAL). This step ensures that only genuinely unnecessary deliberation is targeted for compression, preventing the removal of critical reasoning or the final answer.

Regenerating verified shorter continuations from frozen states

From each confirmed overthinking state, the model samples multiple new continuations. The shortest continuation that produces a correct answer is selected as the 'chosen' response. If no valid shorter continuation exists, the state is discarded.

Constructing state-conditioned preference triples

The system pairs the frozen reasoning state with the shorter regenerated continuation (chosen) and the original longer continuation (rejected). Pairs are filtered to ensure sufficient token savings and fit within length limits, creating a dataset of local preferences.

Optimizing the model with Direct Preference Optimization

The base model is fine-tuned using DPO on the constructed preference pairs. This teaches the model to prefer the shorter, correct continuation over the original redundant one when reaching the same reasoning state, effectively learning when to conclude.
